UPI agent payments, governed — deterministically.

India is building the rails for AI agents to pay on UPI. NPCI's position is clear: "AI may recommend, but authentication and final settlement must follow deterministic auditable rules." SpendVeto is that rule layer. It decides whether an agent's UPI mandate debit may proceed — using the RBI e-mandate framework's own thresholds — and the agent can never approve itself.

What's real, with sources

RBI Digital Payments E-mandate Framework, 2026 (issued 21 April 2026; cards, PPIs and UPI): additional-factor authentication at registration and first debit; recurring debits need no further authentication up to ₹15,000 (₹1,00,000 for insurance, mutual funds and credit-card bills); customers are notified at least 24 hours before each debit; mandates can be withdrawn.

Sources: Economic Law Practice summary · Razorpay

NPCI's Unified Agent Protocol is in development — agents would be registered, verified and given scoped, limited authority, building on UPI Circle. It needs RBI approval before launch. NPCI's chairman, at GFF 2026: "Decision making and execution must remain separate."

Sources: MediaNama · BW Businessworld

Try the rules

This runs SpendVeto's actual UPI evaluator in your browser — the same file the server uses. Change a mandate and watch the verdict.

What this is not. SpendVeto does not initiate, collect or settle any UPI payment, is not a payment aggregator or PSP, and claims no NPCI or RBI approval or certification. "Requires approval" means a human completes the customer's authentication. The UPI settlement rail is a declared, refusing slot until NPCI's agent protocol is live and RBI-cleared. Thresholds are the framework's published defaults — verify them against the RBI text before production. Payee VPAs are masked in every decision (data minimisation under the DPDP Act, 2023). Self-hosting keeps all data on your own infrastructure, in the region you choose.
